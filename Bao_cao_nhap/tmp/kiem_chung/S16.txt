Series arc fault detection via arcing physical characteristics-guided feature
extraction: a dual-signal multi-timescale method
Zhipeng He , Hu Zhao * , Da Li , Weilin Li
School of Automation, Northwestern Polytechnical University, Xi’an, Shaanxi 710129, China




A R T I C L E I N F O                                     A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                 Series arc faults (SAFs) are a primary cause of electrical fires in building distribution systems, requiring reliable
Series arc fault                                          detection to mitigate fire risks and ensure operational safety. Existing methods, though effective under simplified
Electrical fire                                           scenarios, suffer from false alarms and missed detections in complex scenarios involving diverse load types,
Building distribution system
                                                          circuit topologies, arc-generating modes, and arc-like conditions. To address the issues, this paper proposes a SAF
Arcing characteristic
Multi-timescale feature extraction
                                                          detection method that integrates a dual-signal multi-timescale feature extraction framework, an arc fault deci­
Fault detection                                           sion tree, and arc fault decision criteria. Firstly, an experimental platform simulating SAFs in building distri­
                                                          bution systems is established, and the arcing characteristics are analyzed through key electrical parameters—arc
                                                          voltage, current, and zero-sequence current coupling signal—across four stages: arc-igniting, arcing, arc-
                                                          extinguishing, and zero-current under different arc-generating modes. Secondly, based on these characteris­
                                                          tics, with a particular focus on the current and zero-sequence current coupling signal, a comprehensive arc fault
                                                          feature set is constructed by extracting features from both single-cycle and multi-cycle perspectives. Thirdly, the
                                                          arc fault decision tree is trained using the constructed feature set, and the arc fault decision criteria are designed
                                                          based on the persistence characteristics of arc faults. Finally, the superiority of the proposed method is
                                                          demonstrated through offline tests, online verification on a self-developed prototype, anti-interference experi­
                                                          ments, and a comparative analysis. Experimental results show that the proposed method achieves a detection
                                                          accuracy of 99.20 % under test conditions encompassing thirteen loads, four circuit topologies, and two arc-
                                                          generating modes, outperforming five representative benchmark methods under identical test conditions.
                                                          Furthermore, during 360 online tests covering both training conditions and previously unseen real-world
                                                          operating conditions, the proposed method satisfied standard detection time requirements in 354 tests while
                                                          maintaining zero false alarms under arc-like conditions such as load switching.

       from arc voltage, current, and ZSCCS demonstrate that poor-
       contact arcs exhibit stronger transient pulses, while insulation-
       aging arcs display more prominent high-frequency fluctuations
       and stochastic behavior. These findings provide a physical
       foundation for constructing a discriminative feature set to reli­
       ably detect SAFs from both origins.
   (2) Proposes a dual-signal multi-timescale feature extraction frame­
       work for SAF detection. Specifically, seven single-cycle features
       are extracted from the current and ZSCCS to quantify the tran­
       sient pulse, high-frequency fluctuation, and stochastic properties
       of arc faults. Furthermore, to explicitly capture the randomness
       over a longer timescale, four of these features are selected, and
       their standard deviations across multiple consecutive cycles are
       computed to form a set of multi-cycle features. Validated under a
       comprehensive set of conditions encompassing thirteen loads,
       four circuit topologies, and two arc-generating modes, the con­
       structed feature set achieves a detection accuracy of 99.20 %
       (9285/9360), substantially outperforming five state-of-the-art
       benchmark methods under identical test conditions.
   (3) Designs a two-stage decision logic employing a decision tree
       followed by definitive criteria. The decision tree is first trained on
       the constructed feature set to provide a preliminary state
       assessment. The arc fault decision criteria, leveraging the
       persistence characteristic of arc faults, then discriminate genuine
       faults from arc-like interference. Extensive validation on a self-
       developed AFDD, covering training conditions and unseen real-
       world conditions, demonstrates a 98.33 % (354/360) pass rate
       for detection timeliness and zero false alarms under arc-like
       conditions such as load switching.

distribution systems, arc-like conditions—such as load switching, plug
insertion, and plug removal—can generate waveform signatures similar
to SAFs, potentially causing the arc fault decision tree model to
misclassify them as SAFs. To mitigate the risk of misoperation caused by
these arc-like conditions, additional arc fault decision criteria are
applied after the arc fault decision tree model. Unlike transient arc-like
conditions, SAFs exhibit persistent characteristics. Therefore, if more
than thr12 out of thr11 consecutive time windows are flagged as sus­
pected SAFs, the circuit is determined to have experienced a genuine
SAF.
    According to the IEC 62606 standard, an AFDD must interrupt the
fault circuit within 1000 ms, 500 ms, 250 ms, 150 ms, and 120 ms for
arcing currents below 2.5A, 5A, 10A, 16A, and 32A. Consequently, the
values of parameters thr11 and thr12 are determined based on the root
mean square of the current signal (IRMS).
    In this study, the signal sampling time window is set to 20 ms. In line
with the maximum detection time allowed by the IEC 62606 standard,
the parameter thr11 takes predefined values of 50, 25, 12, 8, and 6 when
IRMS equals 2.5A, 5A, 10A, 16A, and 32A, respectively. For any IRMS
between these discrete points, thr11 is determined by linear interpola­
tion, with the specific calculation given in (21). In real-world distribu­
tion systems, most loads complete their startup within a single time
window. However, certain loads, such as fluorescent lamps, require
multiple time windows to start. Notably, fluorescent lamps operate at
low currents, allowing for a longer detection time. In this study, the
value of thr12 is selected as half of the value of thr11.
