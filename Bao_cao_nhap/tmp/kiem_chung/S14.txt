Non-intrusive series arc fault detection for unknown scenarios based on
differential current and synthetic arcing data
Wenpeng Luan , Hanju Cai , Bochao Zhao *, Yang Han
School of Electrical and Information Engineering, Tianjin University, 300072 Tianjin, China




A R T I C L E I N F O                                       A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                   Series arc fault (SAF) detection is essential for the safety of residential power systems. However, in non-intrusive
Series arc fault detection                                  detection based on trunk currents, SAF signatures are typically masked by other loads operating in parallel. The
Differential current                                        difficulty of extracting robust fault features under such load interference and the data distribution shift between
Synthetic arcing data
                                                            different domains make models trained in specific scenarios ineffective in unknown ones. To address this issue,
Unknown scenario
                                                            this paper proposes a differential current extraction method based on dynamic baseline subtraction to provide
                                                            signals with suppressed masking-load interference for SAF detection, improving the signal-to-noise ratio for
                                                            learning intrinsic fault features and reducing the distribution gap across domains. Furthermore, an interpretable
                                                            SAF data synthesis method based on dimension-wise class activation map is developed in synergy with differ­
                                                            ential currents to overcome the unavailability of real fault data in unknown scenarios for model adaptation. This
                                                            method generates realistic SAF samples by fusing SAF signatures from the source domain with target-domain-
                                                            specific background signals. Subsequently, a dual-branch neural network is employed as an effective classifier
                                                            to validate the proposed methods, and it is further compressed through knowledge distillation via knowledge
                                                            review to enable practical deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Experimental results demonstrate
                                                            that the proposed methods substantially enhance the cross-domain generalization, raising SAF detection accu­
                                                            racy in unknown scenarios from 66.2% to 96.4%. Moreover, the lightweight model achieves a detection time of
                                                            59.8 ms on the Raspberry Pi 4B, meeting the IEC 62606 standard with a negligible accuracy loss of 0.7%
                                                            compared with the full-sized model. Notably, when integrated with existing methods, the proposed methods
                                                            improve their average accuracy by 26.8% in unknown scenarios, demonstrating broad universality.
