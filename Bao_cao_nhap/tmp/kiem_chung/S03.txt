1. Introduction

    Developing reliable arc fault detectors is a major issue in pro-
tecting AC domestic installations. Indeed, in the United States and
Europe, several studies have shown that a signiﬁcant proportion of
domestic ﬁres are caused by arcing faults [1,2]. Unlike the United
States, where these circuits are imposed in homes, this is not the
case in the Europe (EEC). However, new European Regulations (IEC
62606) [3] will make them compulsory in the coming years. Cur-
rently, some devices are available which are suitable for detection
in 230 V/50 Hz AC power installations.

rent literature, are based on an analysis of the line current. Several

                                                                                         the current. The current is ﬁrst ﬁltered by a notch ﬁlter centered on
                                                                                         zero frequency which, by attenuating the fundamental component
                                                                                         f0 , highlights the differences between two successive periods. The
                                                                                         coefﬁcient r has the advantage of being very sensitive to the current
                                                                                         amplitude when it exceeds the noise level. Our tests show a normal
                                                                                         value of r which is very stable and above 0.94 for currents above
                                                                                         250 mA. In the presence of an arc and irrespective of the load (linear

                                                                                              The detection algorithm includes two main modules. The ﬁrst
                                                                                         calculates the coefﬁcient r and the second is an FIR ﬁlter which
                                                                                         estimates the derivative of r in real time. The estimation is per-
                                                                                         formed by an algebraic approach that is very resistant to noise. The
                                                                                         time response of the algorithm, approximately half a current period
                                                                                         (10 ms in Europe), largely exceeds the requirements imposed by the
                                                                                         various standards.
                                                                                              The detection method is currently implemented on an embed-
                                                                                         ded system based around an ARM microcontroller. Current
                                                                                         measurement is made by a single toroidal passive probe. Capable
                                                                                         of being operated with currents of low amplitude and at moder-
                                                                                         ate cost, this type of detector can be applied to a large number of
                                                                                         electrical safety applications.
